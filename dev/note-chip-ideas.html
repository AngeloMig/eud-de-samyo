"Smells like": 10 chip ideas

Today: four round chips with an illustration and the note name, all treated the same. Ideas below add meaning (which notes come first, how strong), make them more readable, or more useful for research. Uses Dior Homme Intense's notes. Hover or tap where it says so.

0Now

Illustrated pills, equal weight, no order.

  • Lavender
  • Iris
  • Vetiver
  • Cedar

1Grouped as a pyramidrecommended

Top / Heart / Base rows show what you smell first and what lasts. Comes straight from the top/middle/base note fields; the core idea of fragrance research.

Top
  • Lavender
Heart
  • Iris
Base
  • Vetiver
  • Cedar

2Strength dotsexample data

Three small dots per note show how prominent it is. Only from a real source (brand material or your own wear test), never guessed.

  • Lavender
  • Iris
  • Vetiver
  • Cedar

3Real ingredient photos

Small round photos instead of drawings (like the hero's lemon, cinnamon, vanilla cut-outs): more luxurious and real. Needs a photo per note in the note metaobject.

  • Lemon
  • Cinnamon
  • Vanilla

4Coloured by scent family

Each chip takes its family's colour (aromatic = lavender, floral = lilac, earthy = sage, woody = sand). The palette starts to "say" the scent at a glance and ties in with the Shop by scent tiles.

  • Lavender
  • Iris
  • Vetiver
  • Cedar

5Tap a note to learn it

Each chip is a button; tapping shows one plain sentence about the note below the row. Great for beginners, works on phones (no hover needed).

Lavender: clean, herbal and a little sweet, like fresh laundry. Here it opens the perfume.

6Big illustrated tiles

Square tiles with a large drawing and the name under it: the illustrations finally get room to be seen. Takes more space; best on the product page.

  • Lavender
  • Iris
  • Vetiver
  • Cedar

7One sentence instead of chips

Reads like a perfumer describing it; the notes are underlined and carry tiny icons. Most editorial, fits the NOHO tone.

Opens with lavender, turns powdery with iris, and dries down to vetiver and cedar.

8On a wear timeline

Notes placed on a line by when you smell them: on spraying, after an hour, after a few hours. Teaches how perfume develops. Times are typical, not measured.

  1. SprayLavender
  2. ~1 hourIris
  3. 3 hours +Vetiver, Cedar

9Hairline, text only

Thin outlined pills with just the italic name: quiet and very luxury-retail; lets the bottle stay the hero. The illustrations move to the product page.

  • Lavender
  • Iris
  • Vetiver
  • Cedar

10"Also in…" on hover

Hovering (or focusing) a note shows other perfumes in the store with the same note, linking to them. Turns notes into a way to discover.

  • LavenderAlso in: Sauvage, Libre
  • IrisAlso in: Dior Homme
  • VetiverAlso in: Terre d'Hermès
  • CedarAlso in: The One, Terre d'Hermès
